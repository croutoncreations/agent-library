# Hosted Agent Library Copyright Safe Harbor Review

Reviewed November 16, 2026. Internal recommendation and registration preparation; no filing has been made.

I recommend pursuing a U.S. DMCA designated-agent registration and a working notice-and-counter-notice process before broadly launching the hosted service. Agent Library stores user-supplied documents and files, including material supplied by connected agents. Private workspaces do not eliminate the possibility of storing infringing material. This is a recommendation based on the service's function, not a legal determination that every activity qualifies for protection.

## What registration does

Section 512(c) addresses material stored at a user's direction. Registration is one prerequisite for the relevant liability protection; it is not a certificate of blanket immunity and does not license infringement or protect all of the operator's own conduct. Eligibility also depends on handling knowledge of infringement, responding promptly, and the statutory financial-benefit and control conditions. Section 512(i) requires a reasonably implemented repeat-infringer policy and accommodation of qualifying standard technical measures. Section 512(m) does not generally require monitoring all user content to seek infringement. [17 U.S.C. section 512](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#512).

The hosted service's own operator needs to satisfy the relevant conditions. Its infrastructure provider's registration does not substitute for the operator's designation. Before filing a new designation, check whether Crouton Creations, LLC already has a current one covering its services.

## Registration steps

1. Search the [official designated-agent directory](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/) for the LLC and existing product names. If the same LLC already has a current designation, update it rather than creating conflicting entries. Separate legal entities require separate designations.
2. Use the Copyright Office's online system to register or amend the service provider. Supply the LLC's legal name and physical street address and appropriate alternate business, product, and website names, including Agent Library.
3. Choose one agent: an individual, role, department, or outside service. Supply the agent's mailing address, telephone number, and email. A monitored `support@croutonhq.com` can be the email; an email address alone is insufficient. Agent contact details are public. [Required registration information](https://www.copyright.gov/title37/201/37cfr201-38.html).
4. Pay the current $6 designation or amendment fee and publish matching agent contact information on an accessible service page. [Current fee schedule](https://www.copyright.gov/about/fees.html).
5. Keep details current and renew before three years elapse. An amendment or resubmission restarts that period. [Renewal regulation](https://www.copyright.gov/title37/201/37cfr201-38.html).

No additional outside agent is required merely because the company is small. The choice depends on whether the team can reliably monitor the inbox, assess notices, and meet the process requirements. An outside service can help operationally but does not replace the operator's responsibilities.

## Process required alongside registration

Create an intake and escalation procedure for notices identifying the work, disputed material, claimant contact details, signature, and required good-faith and authority statements. Act expeditiously on compliant notices by removing or disabling access, and promptly notify the affected uploader. For private content, an operator must be able to restrict the identified material without exposing unrelated content.

Provide a counter-notice route for mistake or misidentification. A valid counter-notice includes identification of the removed material, signature, a statement under penalty of perjury, and contact, jurisdiction, and service-of-process information. Forward it to the claimant. The statutory restoration process generally calls for reinstatement after 10 to 14 business days unless the claimant reports filing the relevant court action. Record deadlines and the basis for action; escalate contested cases to counsel. [Copyright Office notice-and-takedown guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/512/).

Document and actually enforce a policy for repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances. Avoid making every unverified complaint an automatic strike. Keep enough restricted case history to support decisions and appeals, with an appropriate retention schedule. Counsel should review the procedure and the applicability of section 512 to hosted storage, indexing, previews, and connected-agent actions.

## Publication checklist

- Confirm any existing registration, the chosen agent or agent title, public phone number, and mailing address.
- Complete or update the filing and publish contact details matching the directory; do not label an unfiled contact a registered agent.
- Verify the team can restrict the identified content, notify users, handle counter-notices and restoration, and terminate repeat infringers when appropriate.
- Publish the final copyright section after the contact details and procedure are settled.

The Terms draft contains a copyright complaint route and repeat-infringer language, but does not claim that registration is complete. The filing, any fee, and ongoing operational responsibilities remain a separate owner action.
